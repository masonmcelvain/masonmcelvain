## Making moves

I made some big life changes this summer!

I bought a camper van. And I got a new job.

These are things I've wanted for a year or two now and I'm happy they happened. But it does cause me to reflect on why it took me so long to make them happen.

There are logistical reasons of course - suitable vans and jobs are hard to come by. But that's only part of the story.

**Vision**: Is this even the trajectory I want to take? Do I envision myself as a remote tech worker who lives in a van part time and lives in a home in SLC part time? It's a funny question because I don't exist in a vacuum; the status quo was that I was already a remote tech worker living in SLC full time and living in a Prius occasionally. Nevertheless vision is something I grapple with, and I think it's the most important step because it requires imagination and grounds my actions.

**Investment**: I had to set aside money for a van, decide what my requirements and dealbreakers were, then patiently wait until the unicorn van appeared online. For the job, I had to interview with many companies and learn about the process and what I really wanted for myself.

**Action**: Armed with self-knowledge, I prepared to strike at the first opportunity that checked all the boxes. I definitely had some vans and jobs that looked promising but didn't work out. I'm grateful I wound up where I did as I perceive it to be the best of the options I had, and I wonder how different my life would look if circumstance hadn't steered me from those alternative realities.

This is definitely not the final destination but it was satisfying to exercise agency in my life. Change makes me anxious at times because change always carries risk. However, the experiences I have will teach me more about what I want for myself amidst my affordances and will cause me to repeat the cycle of change one day, further aligning with the truest expression of myself.

For example, I love having a home gym in my shared house in SLC and I would love to own a home with one eventually.

I think I've had enough big changes for now and I welcome the growth that will come with these new pots I've planted myself in.

### Rafting trip

Mango convinced me to raft to a tower and I was skeptical but ended up having so much fun! We rafted from Grand Junction to a tower called "Arch Tower", about 12 miles of active paddling down the lazy Colorado River. The next day we climbed the terrifying tower and rafted out another 13 miles just over the Utah border. The trip was a bit of a suffer-fest but it was so rewarding to create some adventure right in our backyard. My favorite memory is floating down the last few miles of the river with Mango under the stars. It was so tranquil soaking in the sounds of the river and letting it carry our tired bodies as we silently stared into space.
